01

The five rules that decide the event

Everything below is detail. These five points are the event.

Rule 01 — the captain rule Personal occupation time accrues only to the rally captain sitting in the Palace. Joiners get points, rewards and glory, but zero occupation seconds. If your kingdom wants a Tyrant, one named governor must lead the rallies, over and over, for hours. Everyone else is fuel.
Rule 02 — buffs before bodies Aerie buffs are +50% to a single stat, alliance-wide, for 30 minutes. Two aeries held is a bigger swing than 40% more troops. Alliances that sprint at the Palace with no buffs feed the alliances that took the towers first.
Rule 03 — buffs are alliance-scoped An aerie buff and a crystal skill only reach members of the alliance that earned it. This single fact reshapes every two-alliance plan: your partner alliance holding a tower does not buff your Palace garrison. Plan the split around that, not around goodwill.
Rule 04 — where troops die Palace and Aerie fights only injure. Attacking another governor's town kills. The infirmary is unlimited on the battlefield only. City hits are a deliberate, costed decision — never a way to pass the time.
Rule 05 — the exit trap The moment you leave the battlefield, infirmary capacity snaps back to normal and everything above the cap dies. More troops have been lost to logging off than to enemy rallies. Heal down before you leave, and use the +500% Rearmament healing window.
02

Event structure & timeline

Flamedragon Tyrant is an alliance-based, cross-kingdom battlefield. Kingdoms of similar progress are grouped into a Zone; alliances from every kingdom in that Zone compete on one shared map for a single throne. The winner holds the Tyrant title for 30 days over the whole Zone.

Eligibility gates, before anything else. Your kingdom must have unlocked Hero Generation 6 before the cycle begins, or it cannot compete — ineligible kingdoms can still spectate the battles. Your account needs a Town Centre at Truegold IV or above to register as a combatant. The event runs on roughly a two-month cycle, so a kingdom that just missed Gen 6 has a known next window to aim at.

03

Qualification & roster construction

How alliances qualify

  • Every alliance in the Zone may register. Only the top-ranking alliances by power get enlistment eligibility when sign-up closes.
  • The number of qualifying slots scales with how many kingdoms were merged into the Zone. Small Zones typically qualify around 40 alliances; large ones scale to 115 or more.
  • Ranking is measured across the whole Zone, not inside your kingdom. Being #1 at home means nothing if the Zone is stacked.
  • Individual governors need a minimum town-centre / Truegold tier set by the Zone grouping.
Sign-up gamesmanship Alliances near the cutoff line sometimes park strong accounts temporarily, obscure alliance power, or register in the final minutes so rivals can't read the threshold. It works occasionally and locks you out permanently when it doesn't. If you are not comfortably above the historical cutoff, register early and stop being clever.

Choosing your 60

Sixty slots is the hard ceiling, and it is smaller than it feels. A roster built purely from the power leaderboard loses to a roster built from availability.

The map is one shared arena, concentric by design: an outer Border Region where cities land and shields still work, an inner Magma Sanctum where shields are stripped and cannot be re-applied, and the Palace at dead centre ringed by four Aeries.

05

The Palace — and the captain rule

The Palace sits at map centre, shielded for the first hour after the battlefield opens. When it unlocks, the first claimants must first clear the Old Guard NPC defenders before any governor can hold it.

Read this twice Occupation time is credited only to the rally captain who is holding the Palace. Joiners in that same rally accrue nothing. The Tyrant is the governor with the longest total occupation time at 18:00 — and if two governors tie, the one who reached that duration first wins.

What this changes

  • You must name a single throne candidate before D-Day. Two candidates in one alliance splitting time is the most common way a strong alliance ends up with no Tyrant.
  • The candidate should be a high-deployment account that can hold a huge garrison, not necessarily your highest raw power.
  • The captain role can be transferred to another player inside the Palace without leaving the team — occupation time then starts accruing to the new captain. This is your relief valve if the candidate has to step away, and your tool for feeding time to a second player once the throne is safe.
  • The First Occupancy Reward goes to every member of the first alliance to occupy the Palace — not just the captain. This is worth an all-in opening rush even for alliances that cannot hold it.
  • The alliance leaderboard sums the occupation time of all your captains, so a second and third captain still build the alliance ranking after your Tyrant bid is settled.

Holding versus taking

Holding is cheaper than taking, and the Palace favours the incumbent stacked with aerie buffs. The practical consequence: get in early, then convert to defence. An alliance that seizes the Palace at 12:20 with Life + Shield buffs running can survive rallies from opponents with two or three times its power.

The four Aeries

Four Aeries surround the Palace and open at the same moment it does. Each runs a 30 minutes vulnerable / 30 minutes protected cycle, and each keeps its own occupation leaderboard. Whichever alliance dominates an Aerie longest during a vulnerable round receives that Aerie's bonus alliance-wide for 30 minutes.

Confirm the compass on the day Every source agrees the north and south Aeries are the defensive pair (Health, Defense) and the east and west Aeries are the offensive pair (Attack, Lethality). Which named tower sits on which side of that pair is not consistently reported, and some guides describe each pair as granting both of its stats. The table above uses the most common mapping. Open the Battlefield Overview during the first vulnerable round and confirm before you assign squads — what matters is which tower gives the stat you want, not what it is called.
The rhythm most alliances miss You fight for an Aerie during its vulnerable half-hour, and you receive the buff during the protected half-hour that follows. So the Palace push is not simultaneous with the Aerie fight — it comes immediately after it, while the towers are locked and your buff is live. Alternate: even half-hour = take towers. Odd half-hour = hit the throne.
Choosing which towers to contest Contesting all four with 60 combatants means losing all four. Pick two and win them decisively. If you hold the Palace: Life + Shield. If you are trying to take it: Blade + Dragon. Switch the pair the moment your posture switches.

The gatherer's trap Mine fights award no personal points — unlike Palace and Aerie combat, which is where all your points come from. Losing troops to defend a node is a pure loss. Gatherers should retreat rather than contest a stronger squad, take the next node, and stay out of the point-blind zone.

Budgeting the battle

Crystal skills reportedly cost somewhere in the range of 10,000 to 50,000 crystals each. That means your skill count is decided in the first two hours by how many people you assigned to farming — not by how hard you fight.

Where low-power accounts win the event Whales ignore Lv.1 nodes on the outer map because they want to be in the centre. That leaves the entire outer ring to your smaller accounts, who can farm uncontested all afternoon, feed the alliance skill pool, and bank personal points from gathering — all with near-zero troop loss. This is the highest-value job on the roster for a low-power player, and almost nobody assigns it deliberately.
08

Crystal skills — and when to fire them

Only R4/R5 members standing on the battlefield can deploy Crystal Skills, via the Alliance Crystal Skills button. Skills buff every member of that alliance on the battlefield, stack with all other bonuses, run on individual cooldowns, and can be run concurrently. They cannot be deployed once the Palace battle ends, and the crystal pool does not carry over to the next event.

The end-game hoard

The strongest crystal play in the event is not spending early. Around the final 60–90 minutes, leading alliances have burned their healing, their activity drops, and their garrisons are thin. An alliance that has banked 60,000+ crystals and fires Dragonroar Rally → seize → Dragonscale Armor → Dragonblood Healing in sequence at that moment can take the throne from a stronger opponent and hold it to the buzzer. Occupation time is cumulative, but so is exhaustion.

Plan A — the 60-player deployment

A fixed four-group order of battle: 20 in the centre, 10 on Life, 10 on Dragon, 20 farming from four posts. Every position, every rotation, every call.

What this build is Seize early, entrench, grind. It commits to a defensive tower pair and spends nothing on disruption. That makes it very hard to remove from the Palace — and it means you must take the Palace in the first thirty minutes, because you are not built to take it back from an entrenched enemy later.

Why HP + Lethality is the right pair here

The obvious defensive pair is Life + Shield. Life + Dragon is better for this build, and it is worth knowing why before someone argues with you about it.

  • Life Aerie (+50% Health, North) deepens the pool your garrison has to lose before it breaks. It is the single best survival stat because it scales with everything else you stack on top.
  • Dragon Aerie (+50% Lethality, East) works on defence, not only attack. Lethality is what converts your garrison's hits into severe injuries in the attacking rally. Held under Life, every failed enemy rally costs the attacker a real army, not a scratch.
  • Shield (+50% Defense) reduces incoming damage but does nothing to punish the attacker. Against a field of 40+ alliances taking turns at you, making each attempt expensive is worth more than surviving each attempt slightly better — attackers who lose 400k troops on one attempt do not come back a third time.
Contingency If you lose the Palace and have to take it back, the correct pair flips to Blade (W) + Dragon (E) — Dragon stays, Scale abandons Life and swings west. Rehearse that call: "SCALE WEST".

SCALE · 10 · Life Aerie (North)  ·  TALON · 10 · Dragon Aerie (East)

Identical structure, different tower: 1 rally lead, 1 backup lead, 8 joiners, 1 R4/R5 among them. Ten players is roughly one full rally — which is exactly what an Aerie needs, and exactly why you contest two towers and not four.

The rule that makes this build work Aerie squads are not idle during protected half-hours. On the vulnerable round they fight for their tower; the moment the tower locks, all 20 of them join the VANGUARD's Palace rally. Your Palace manpower alternates between 20 and 40 — and the 40-strong window lands on exactly the half-hour when your aerie buffs are live. That is the entire engine of this deployment.
  • Staging: both squads sit on the Palace side of their tower. One short march to the Aerie, one short march to the Palace, no wasted travel either way.
  • Concede fast. If a stronger bloc commits to your tower, take the loss and pull back rather than feeding a losing rally for 25 minutes. Round-based ownership means the next window is only 30 minutes away.
  • Only one squad needs to hold for the buff to matter. If SCALE wins Life but TALON loses Dragon, the Palace still gets +50% HP for the round. Never treat a split round as a failure.
  • The R4/R5 in each squad can fire skills from the tower if the centre's leaders are mid-march.

EMBER · 20 · four farming posts

Recalibrate what "winning" costs

The number that changes the whole plan In a competitive Zone the Palace changes hands constantly, and the winning Tyrant's total cumulative hold is frequently under one hour — with the longest single unbroken hold often under 30 minutes. You are not being asked to hold for six hours. You are being asked to accumulate roughly 45–60 minutes of captain time in bursts, and to be holding more often than anyone else. That is a far more achievable target than it sounds, and it makes the reform-instantly discipline the single highest-value habit in the plan.
  • Every second counts equally. A 4-minute hold at 13:12 is worth the same as a 4-minute hold at 17:50. Never write off a round because the Palace looks contested.
  • Do not expect to be first. First occupancy is decided by march speedups and connection latency, not power — and the first occupier almost never becomes Tyrant. Take the First Occupancy Reward if you get it, then settle into the grind.
  • Share the captaincy once the bid is safe. Strong alliances rotate the captain role inside the Palace late in the battle so several members land in the top-100 occupation ranking. Your 3 relief captains are the beneficiaries — call it around 17:00 once the candidate's lead is comfortable.
Realistic targets for this deployment First Occupancy Reward if the rush lands, roughly 45–60 minutes of cumulative captain time for the candidate, a recorded occupation ranking for the candidate plus 1–3 relief captains, an alliance occupation-leaderboard placing, and full personal-point milestones across all 60. On those numbers the Tyrant title is a live contest, not a fantasy.
11

Plan B — a two-alliance bloc

120 combatants, but two separate buff economies. Handled well this is far more than double. Handled naively it is worse than one alliance, because half your buffs land on people who aren't in the fight.

The constraint that governs everything Aerie bonuses and Crystal Skills apply to the earning alliance's own members only. Alliance B holding the Life Aerie does nothing for Alliance A's Palace garrison. Alliance B's Dragonblood Healing does not heal Alliance A. Any plan that treats the two alliances as one army is wrong at the mechanical level.

First: settle who wears the crown

Occupation time is per-governor. Two alliances each pushing their own candidate means the Palace changes hands between friends all afternoon and both candidates finish with half the time a rival needed. Before D-Day, in writing:

  • One named throne candidate for the whole bloc, in Alliance A.
  • Alliance B's leadership agrees in advance to a compensation package: title appointments from the Tyrant (Bloodwyrm Warlord, Wyrmbreath Overlord, Dragonscale Guard, Dragontooth General — each +20% to a stat plus 2,000 deployment), commendation chests, and priority on the Dragon Banquet.
  • Alliance B feeds a secondary captain occupation time for the occupation ranking once the throne is mathematically safe — usually after 16:30. That's a real, valuable consolation: the top 100–200 occupation slots pay Mythril and gems.

Division of labour

Alliance A — the Throne

  • Holds the Palace, continuously, with the bloc's single candidate as captain.
  • Contests and holds Life (N) + Shield (S) — the defensive pair — because A is the one that has to survive rallies.
  • Its crystal skills are the defensive suite: Dragonscale Armor, Dragonblood Healing, Dragonroar Rally.
  • Runs a smaller farming detail (6–8) because its members must be available as rally bodies.

Alliance B — the Hammer

  • Contests and holds Blade (W) + Dragon (E) — the offensive pair — because B's job is breaking things.
  • Counter-rallies: hits enemy garrisons the moment a rival takes the Palace, so A can re-seize a weakened throne.
  • Denies crystal nodes to the strongest rival bloc, and runs the bloc's large farming detail (14–18).
  • Its crystal skills are the offensive suite: Dragonbreath Fury, Dragonroar Rally, Dragonborn Gathering.
  • Selectively clears enemy cities near the Sanctum to lengthen rival reinforcement marches — the one place city hits are justified, and only with a named call, since these kill troops.

Why B never garrisons the Palace

If Alliance B members join A's throne rally they are simply bodies without A's aerie buffs — and worse, B loses those members from its own tower and counter-rally jobs. B contributes by removing threats, not by adding bodies. The one exception is the final push at 17:00, when maximising raw troop count in the Palace outweighs everything else and B's members can pile into A's rallies as bulk.

Troop loss, infirmary & the exit trap

ActionOutcomePoints?

The most expensive mistake in the event Infirmary capacity is unlimited only while you are on the battlefield. On exit it reverts to your real capacity, and severely injured soldiers above that cap die immediately, with some reserves pushed to the Enlistment Office. Before you leave: heal down, or accept the loss knowingly. Set an alarm for 18:10 if you have to.
  • Alliance members not dispatched to the battlefield can still send healing help to those who are. Put your bench on healing duty for the whole six hours.
  • Champion's Eve alliance preparation milestones grant up to +200% battlefield healing speed and −30% healing cost for your alliance, in six 50,000-point steps. These apply during the battle, unlike the Rearmament bonus.
  • Rearmament (+500% speed, −30% cost) runs from Sunday 18:00 to Tuesday 24:00 UTC. Schedule the alliance's mass heal at a peak-activity hour so healing helps flow fast — stacking it onto an existing rally point like Bear Trap works well, because everyone is already online and tapping.
  • Healing still costs resources. A six-hour grind can drain more than a full KvK — stockpile in the Eve of Battle phase, not on the Monday after.

Points, rankings & rewards

Where personal points come from

Personal points are earned only by attacking governors or NPCs garrisoned in the Palace and the Aeries, plus gathering crystals and killing escorts. Occupation by itself pays nothing directly, and neither do town raids or node scraps. In practice a disciplined player who never sees the Palace can still hit high milestones through escorts and gathering alone.

Two F2P routes

There are two low-risk ways to farm this event, and they suit different accounts. Run whichever fits, or alternate.

Route 1 — the small structures

Sit near the centre and hit the small buildings ringing the Palace on repeat. Because Palace and Aerie combat only injures and the battlefield infirmary is unlimited, you can grind points here almost indefinitely without losing a soldier permanently. This is the highest point-per-hour route available to a low-power account.

Risk: you are inside the Magma Sanctum with no shield. Expect to get farmed by whales between rounds. Accept it — the injuries are recoverable.

Route 2 — the outer ring

Stay in the Border Region with a shield up. Farm Lv.1 nodes and escorts all afternoon, join the Palace rally only on a called full push. Fewer points than Route 1, near-zero exposure, and it is the route that feeds your alliance's crystal pool.

Risk: almost none, provided you concede contested nodes instead of fighting for them.

Either route, same closing sequence Hit every personal-point milestone. Place all three predictions plus the free trivia. Heal down before leaving the map. You finish with Mythril, gems, speedups and an intact army.
Set your ranking expectations honestly Community guides disagree here. Some report the top 100–200 occupation slots as easy to sneak into; the official wiki's own F2P note is blunter — outside top-tier servers, climbing past roughly rank 1000 on the personal ranking is hard without real investment. Both can be true, because the occupation ranking and the personal-point ranking are different tables with different competition. Plan around the fixed milestones, which are guaranteed; treat every leaderboard placing as a bonus.

The prediction market — free money, played correctly

Predictions are the highest return-per-minute activity in the whole event, and most players either skip them or treat them as a loyalty vote. They are neither.

The wager loop Placing a single 1,000-mark wager completes a Searing Path mission that pays back 500 Dragonclaw Marks and 15 Dragon Essence. A losing bet also refunds 500 marks. So your first wager costs you nothing at all and hands you Dragon Essence on top. There is no version of this where not betting is correct.
  • Back up to three alliances at 1,000 marks each. A correct call returns 10,000.
  • You are not obliged to back your own alliance, and there is no gameplay benefit to loyalty. Bet on who will win.
  • If you don't know the power structures in the other kingdoms in your Zone, read the public voting percentages before committing.
  • Trivia predictions are free. Answer all of them; each correct answer is 1,000 marks.
  • Spend leftover marks in the Prediction Shop before the event closes — they do not carry over.

The Dragon Banquet

  • One banquet per reign, kingdom-wide, hosted in the Tyrant's own kingdom. It cannot be cancelled once started and runs for exactly one hour.
  • Governors need Town Centre Lv.13 or above to join, and have 3 dining squads to dispatch.
  • Likes are given during Rearmament — 5 per governor per day, each granting a gift, and only to your own Zone's Tyrant.
  • The Tyrant may edit the Tyrant Declaration once per day; each edit fires a ticker broadcast.

Schedule the banquet at your kingdom's peak hour. One non-cancellable hour, and everyone below TC13 is locked out — announce it in advance.

Pre-battle checklist

T−7 days · preview

  • Read the Zone list. Identify the two or three alliances that can realistically win it.
  • Decide: are you bidding for the throne, or maximising guaranteed rewards? Tell the roster which.
  • If bidding, name the throne candidate. In writing. No second candidate.

Mon–Tue · sign-up

  • Roster locked before registration closes — anyone joining after is disqualified.
  • Alliance registered by R4/R5. Do not leave it to the last hour unless you are deliberately hiding power.

Wed–Thu · dispatch

  • Publish the 60 and the reserve list.
  • Confirm every combatant is available for the full 12:00–18:00 UTC block.
  • Place all three championship predictions. Answer the free trivia predictions.
  • If running a two-alliance bloc: sign the throne agreement and the title-compensation package now.

Fri–Sun · eve of battle

  • Push Champion's Eve alliance preparation points toward all six healing milestones.
  • Stockpile healing resources and speedups. Assume two full army resets.
  • Publish rally ratios by hero generation and pin them.
  • Assign roles by name: throne pool, aerie squads (which two towers), farming detail, relief captains.
  • Set up comms. One voice channel, one calls channel, UTC only.
  • Agree non-aggression pacts with rival kingdoms where possible.
  • Bench members briefed on healing-help duty.

Sunday · D-Day

  • 10:45 — everyone online. No squads scouting or marching, barricade not burning, or you cannot enter.
  • 11:00 — enter. Five-minute lock. Post the call order.
  • 11:05 — teleport by power tier. Candidate and throne pool into the Lava Shelter band.
  • 11:52 — pre-fill the throne rally.
  • 12:00 — seize.
  • 17:00 — burn everything.
  • 18:00 — heal down below your real infirmary cap. Nobody retreats without a call.
  • 18:00 Sun – 24:00 Tue — mass heal under Rearmament. Claim Tyrant's Gift, likes, banquet.
